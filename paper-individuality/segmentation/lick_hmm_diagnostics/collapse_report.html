Why Lick Fits Collapse

The lick trace is binary and 95% zeros, so both Poisson states land on almost the same rate and EM gives up. Binning to 200 ms restores the contrast.

Occupancy of exactly 1.000 and a dwell time equal to the whole session are the same fact stated twice: every frame went into one state, so there is no segmentation at all. The emission rates say why — 0.071 against 0.071 licks per frame is not a distinction a likelihood can find.

What else was tried

bernoulli emissions — rescued 1 of 5
Correctly specified for a 0/1 signal, but it swaps one degeneracy for another: on four of the five sessions it produced 17 000+ segments at a 67 ms median dwell, labelling individual lick frames rather than bouts. The distribution family was never the problem.
500 ms bins — passes the screens, loses the signal
Occupancy and dwell look respectable, but bits_LL falls to 0.004–0.026 against ~0.18 for a working fit. Coarser bins average away the structure they are meant to segment. 200 ms was the best of those tested.
upsampling back to 30 Hz — costs ~100 ms of onset precision
A 200 ms bin carries one label, so an onset is quantised to the bin edge: 200 ms worst case, 58 ms RMS if placed at bin centres. Measured against a frame-resolution decode, onsets moved by a median of 100 ms while bout counts held (1361 → 1329). Fitting coarse and decoding fine recovers the timing.
